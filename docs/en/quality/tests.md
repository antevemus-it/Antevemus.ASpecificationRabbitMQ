# Tests

The suite runs with PHPUnit in three groups, `Unit`, `Contract` and `Broker`, in the CI of every push on PHP 8.2, 8.3 and 8.4, against a RabbitMQ 3.13 started as a service of the workflow:

```bash
docker run -d -p 5672:5672 rabbitmq:3.13
RABBITMQ_HOST=127.0.0.1 RABBITMQ_PORT=5672 vendor/bin/phpunit
```

The broker is read from `RABBITMQ_HOST`, `RABBITMQ_PORT`, `RABBITMQ_USER`, `RABBITMQ_PASSWORD` and `RABBITMQ_VHOST` (127.0.0.1, 5672, guest, guest, `/`). A test that needs it is skipped, with the reason, when it cannot be reached; the CI runs with `--fail-on-skipped`, so there none is skipped. Every test declares exchanges and queues of its own, under unique names, and removes them at the end.

## What is tested

- **The contract battery of the core**, the one every broker satellite runs: `MessagingContractTestCase` of `antevemus/aspecification` (since 2.15.0, in `Antevemus\ASpecification\Messaging\Testing`), which `RabbitMqContractTest` extends and answers with a bench over the real broker. The core runs the same battery against its in-memory reference, so this suite runs it against RabbitMQ only. Each scenario goes the way of an application: the core's `MessagePublisher` into its `InMemoryMessageOutbox`, the core's `OutboxRelay` through the transport, the core's `MessageConsumer` over the source with its `InMemoryProcessedMessageStore` and a transaction manager; it compares the transcript (each delivery's message, outcome and delivery count), the effects of the handlers, the reasons and the dead letters with literals. What it compares is what the contract promises: the order within an ordering key, and the multiset of the rest. The scenarios: a round trip that keeps every field to the microsecond, every consumer gets every message, the order of a key across message names and across batches, a requeued delivery that comes back before the later ones of its key (in one run, with deliveries left behind, one delivery at a time), a dead letter after `maxDeliveries` with its reason and count, bodies that are not messages dead-lettered and never dropped, a name without a handler, a consumer of one tenant, payloads of exactly 65 535 bytes, batches of mixed keys and of many keys, duplicates absorbed by the inbox, a relay that dies between the confirmation and the mark, the deliveries of a dead consumer handed to its successor (twice), a process started again that gets back what it left in flight, a lost connection that fails the whole batch, the secrecy of every reason, report and exception, each bench a world of its own, and a property sequence over random interleavings (six fixed seeds × 150 steps of publications, relays that die or lose the connection, consumers that die before an acknowledgement, handlers that fail once, twice or always, and copies stored by the broker). Every scenario is mandatory and none is skipped. The contract item by item, and how a satellite wires the battery: [Writing a satellite](https://github.com/antevemus-it/Antevemus.ASpecification/blob/main/docs/en/messaging/writing-a-satellite.md), in the documentation of the core.
- **The bench** (`tests/Support/RabbitMqBench.php`), the `IMessagingBench` over RabbitMQ: per instance, exchanges of unique names, and per consumer a quorum queue of a unique name bound by the message names it reads, with its dead letter queue, all removed at the end; a successor and a process started again are the same thing on RabbitMQ, a new connection on the queue of the consumer, made once the socket of the dead one is cut (no `connection.close`, as a killed process) and the broker gave back what it held; a lost connection is a transport whose socket is cut as soon as it opens; a raw body is published to the exchange with the message name as routing key; the dead letters are read from the dead letter queue, with the headers `aspec-dead-reason` and `aspec-dead-delivery-count`; the wait is 0, since a confirmed message is in the queue when `send()` returns.
- **Failures that are RabbitMQ's own**, against the broker: a consumer whose socket is cut after its commit and before its acknowledgement (the acknowledgement fails with the source's exception; everything it held goes, in order and with `x-delivery-count` raised, to the next consumer; the committed message is a duplicate); a broker that cannot be reached (the reason names the broker as the connector describes it, never the password); a message no queue is bound for (failed by the mandatory flag, `312 NO_ROUTE`, sent again once a queue is bound); a nack of a full queue; a missing exchange and the reconnection; and the AMQP headers of a dead letter and the error of a connection, which never repeat the payload nor the password.
- **What the broker does**, measured so that the design rests on it: a quorum queue of RabbitMQ 3.13 puts a message rejected with requeue at the tail (the reason the source keeps a requeued delivery), gives the deliveries of a closed connection back at the head, in order, and counts the deliveries in `x-delivery-count`; a classic queue gives only the `redelivered` flag.
- **The source and the transport on their own**, against the broker and against a scripted one (mocked php-amqplib channels): the pull, its wait and its interval, the delivery count, the requeue kept in order, the dead letter and its confirm (acknowledged only after it), the properties and headers of a published message, each answer of the broker mapped to its result, the connection kept until it fails, the arguments and their messages.
- **The documentation**: every PHP block of `docs/en` and `docs/pt-BR`, and the Quick Start of each README, runs with the answers written in its comments (the blocks that talk to a broker against the test broker); both languages carry the same code and the same files.

## Mutation testing

Infection runs over the whole `src/` in a scheduled workflow, against a RabbitMQ service, with minimum scores one point below the last measure. Measured for 1.0.0, and again for 1.0.1 with the battery of the core, with the same result: 274 mutants, 269 killed, 1 time-out, 4 escaped, all equivalent (constants of the clock of the wait off by one part in a million or a thousand, and a remaining time of exactly zero nanoseconds); MSI 98.54%, mutation code coverage 100%.

The suite has 164 tests (98 run and 66 skipped without a broker).
